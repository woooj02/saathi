Saathi app map

A take-home guide to the code: what the pieces are, one action traced through them, and where to start a change.

What it is

A clinic uploads its fillable English intake PDF. Saathi turns every box into a plain question in English and Nepali. A Nepali-speaking patient opens a link, tells their story once, answers what's left by voice, typing or tapping, and the clinic gets its own PDF back, filled in English, by email.

Server (app/)

FastAPI. One file per part: reading and filling the PDF, Gemini calls, dates, email, storage.

Pages (web/)

Plain HTML, CSS and JS, no build step. fill.js is the patient, clinic.js the clinic.

Gemini

Converts a form, listens to answers and stories, reads questions aloud. Code checks everything it returns.

Storage

One folder per form: the PDF, form.json, and its Nepali recordings. Nothing about the patient is kept.

One action: the patient tells their story

The route, three stops

Open the project in your editor and start it with ./run.sh. Then go to http://localhost:8010/f/sample, choose नेपाली, press सुरु गर्नुहोस्, and tap the mic on the story screen.

  1. web/js/fill.js, search for function story(
    The story screen. Tapping the mic calls recordAnswer() (in web/js/voice.js) with a longer quiet gap than a single answer, because older patients pause to think. The recording is posted to /api/forms/<id>/story.
  2. app/story.py, extract() and _schema()
    One Gemini call gets the recording plus every question that can be answered by voice. The JSON schema is built from the form itself, so Gemini can only name real questions and real option values.
  3. app/story.py, _check(), then back to storyResult() in fill.js
    Each answer goes through the same checks as a single answer: dates are converted by code (app/nepali_dates.py), phone numbers formatted, tap answers matched to the PDF's options. Anything that doesn't hold up is dropped. The phone shows what was filled, both ways, and openIndex() skips those questions afterwards.

A practice to reuse

Name the riskiest unknown, test it cheaply first, and write the fallback down before you build.

The spec (devpost/spec.md › The useful unknown: how well Nepali speech is recognised) said Chrome's free Nepali recognition might not work, planned a small test page (/lab, build step 1), and wrote the fallback in advance: record the answer and have Gemini listen. The test came back with no words for any of the 6 answers. Because the fallback was already agreed, switching took one change (interpret_audio() in app/answers.py), and that same "Gemini listens" pipeline is what made the story possible. On the next project: find the part you're least sure works, spend ten minutes proving or disproving it before building on top of it, and decide the plan B while it's still cheap.

Run it again

cp .env.example .env, add your keys (see README.md), then ./run.sh and open http://localhost:8010/clinic or /f/sample. Tests: ./run.sh test.
